# Pricing research and decision memo

Decision, October 2, 2026: retain the MIT-licensed preview with no paid offer. Zero pricing interviews or purchase observations have occurred. Do not turn stated curiosity into demand.

Potential paid value to investigate later: dependable convenience, setup/support and supported delivery. Licensing rights in the existing [MIT license](../../LICENSE) remain clear. No fee buys exact action times, private access or relationship certainty.

## Neutral interview questions

Ask after comprehension, without naming competitor prices or suggesting a number first:

1. Which part, if any, was useful? What would you use instead?
2. What setup or support would make this practical for you?
3. If a future release reliably handled a chosen daily check and confirmed-event delivery, what would that convenience replace? That capability is not this preview's offer.
4. Would you prefer self-hosting, help with setup, or a managed option? What concerns affect that choice?
5. What, if anything, would you consider paying for support or dependable convenience? Why? Record a range only if volunteered.
6. What would make you decline at any price?
7. May we contact you for a later explicitly described offer? Contact consent is separate from notes consent.

Capture their exact voluntary words privately. Separate interest, hypothetical price, offer accepted, paid transaction and later retained use. There is no checkout or deposit in this study.

## Decision rule

Use comprehension/setup findings first. If supported delivery is unavailable or setup repeatedly blocks users, improve the product route before pricing. Summarize patterns and counterexamples with denominators; do not declare a market price from 5-10 interviews.

An actual paid experiment needs a supported release, clear offer/refund terms, an explicitly approved account and billing action. This run creates none. No subscriptions, lifetime deals, payment collection or paid certainty claims.
